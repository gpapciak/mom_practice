Laptop Check

For a helper to run once, in Safari, on the MacBook this will be used on. It measures the laptop, not the person — nothing here is a test of anyone.

Before you start: do not change Safari’s zoom, the window size, or the screen resolution. The whole point is to record how the machine is set up right now, at the size it is actually used at.

Step 2 · needs you

Physical size

Hold any credit or bank card flat against the screen, next to the blue rectangle. Use the buttons until the rectangle is exactly as wide as the card.

Every bank card in the world is 85.6 mm wide, so matching one tells us how many screen pixels make a millimetre. That is what turns the layout into real visual angle instead of a guess.

Step 3 · needs you

Viewing distance

Roughly how far are the user's eyes from the screen in normal use, in centimetres? A rough number is genuinely fine — 50 to 60 cm is typical for a laptop at a table.

Step 4 · needs you

Mouse movement

Click inside the box, then wiggle the mouse around inside it for a few seconds until it stops by itself.

Click here, then move the mouse

This checks how often Safari reports mouse movement on this machine. It decides whether thinking time can be separated from reaching time — the one measurement the whole design is most careful about.

Step 5 · needs you

Sound

Turn the volume up, then press the button. You should hear a short sentence.

If you hear nothing but the table below says it started, the Mac’s own volume is down or muted. The browser cannot tell the difference, which is exactly why a person has to run this step.
